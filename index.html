What the pattern shows

Employees who exit share a consistent profile: younger, single, working overtime, without an equity stake, and reporting poor work-life balance. Employees with none of these conditions leave at rates well below the company average, while employees with two or more of these conditions leave at rates approaching one in three.

Compensation matters, but not in the way a simple raise would fix. The income gap tracks tenure and job level more than it tracks pay policy — leavers are concentrated in early-career, lower-band roles rather than being underpaid relative to peers at the same level. Equity participation, by contrast, shows a sharp and consistent link to retention regardless of income band.

Sales Representatives at 39.8% Attrition

Sales Reps suffer an alarming 39.8% exit rate (33 departures of 83 headcount)—nearly 2.5× the enterprise baseline (16.1%). Driven by aggressive targets, lowest median base salary in the company ($2,626/mo), and high early-tenure turnover.

Career Stagnation Tenure Disillusionment

29 Long-Tenured Leavers (7–15 Yrs No Promo)

A distinct anomaly cohort of 29 veteran employees (7–15+ years at TP) exited after enduring 7 to 15 consecutive years without a single promotion. Even senior staff (Research Directors & Managers with up to 40 years of service) left due to career dead-ends.

Promotion Freeze: 7 – 15 Yrs Without Promo
Impacted Cohort: 29 High-Tenure Exits
Toxic Compound Squeeze Comp & Workload

Low-Salary & Overtime Compounding Trap

In Laboratory Technicians (avg $3,237) and Sales Reps ($2,626), employees subjected to mandatory overtime combined with zero stock options experience catastrophic attrition rates exceeding 50%. Overtime alone triples exit risk (30.5% vs 10.4%).

Overtime Risk Surge: 30.5% vs 10.4% (3.0×)
Zero Equity Attrition: 24.4% (154 Leavers)

Strategic Recommendations

Where to focus retention investment

Priority One

Reduce overtime load in Sales and Lab roles

These two roles combine the highest headcount exposure and the strongest single behavioral driver of exit.

Priority Two

Extend equity to zero-option employees

Any level of stock participation is associated with roughly a two-thirds reduction in exit rate.

Priority Three

Protect early-tenure, single employees

This group carries the highest combined risk profile and the shortest average tenure before exit.
